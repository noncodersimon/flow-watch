# Household OS - design panel findings

Companion to ARCHITECTURE.md. Three architecture proposals (security-first, lean-founder, product-first) were judged by two independent judges and then attacked by four adversarial reviewers (regulatory sceptic, security red team, cost and operations sceptic, agent-buildability sceptic) on 3 September 2026. The findings below are verbatim from the panel and are the source of most of the "must change" items in the architecture. Severities are the reviewers' own.

---
JUDGE 1  winner: security-first
  security-first: total 54  sec 9 cost 7 speed 6 product 7 reg 9 build 8 evolve 8
    The only proposal that turns 'demonstrably' into five properties each proven by a named test on every commit and dated on a public page: FORCED RLS with a restrictive boundary, per-household DEK with AAD binding and blob encryption before S3 (so erasure is key destruction, provable across versions, replicas and backups), a single custody boundary for KMS and sb_secret, a fixture-tested intake triage, and an erasure job that is itself a test. Regulatory coverage is the most complete (SavingsClaim type, policy modes, PECR template test, DMCC notices, Article 9 flag, Apple 5.1.2(i) in the flow). Loses a point for proposing the Batch API under a ZDR claim (Batch is not ZDR-eligible) and for long-lived IAM keys on an unverified 'Fly has no OIDC' premise. Slowest to real households (private beta Jan-Feb 2027), highest 100k bill, and the household model is thinner than product-first's.
  lean-founder: total 52  sec 7 cost 9 speed 8 product 6 reg 7 build 8 evolve 7
    Best cost discipline (GBP 116 at launch, one AWS account, no Cloudflare) and the right build order: Simon's own household on real data at week 4, ingestion after. Its intake_worker role with execute-only security-definer functions is the best least-privilege idea on the panel. But blobs are SSE-KMS only behind presigned URLs, privileged code is split across Edge Functions and Lambdas, the manual test is a web-only Cobalt run until a trigger that its own phase 5 already fires, and outside households forward real bills in weeks 5-7 before the privacy notice, complaints form and finalised DPIA exist - the transparency gap an ICO investigation would find first. Twenty free frontier-model calls a month is a larger abuse and perimeter surface than needed. Ten weeks is optimistic given non-code long poles (D-U-N-S, SES production access, fixtures).
  product-first: total 54  sec 7 cost 7 speed 6 product 9 reg 8 build 8 evolve 9
    The strongest product thinking: six moments, people without logins, viewer scope per property, the supersedes chain, attention_items driving the calm header, notifications carrying a PECR classification the send path enforces, a junior role designed in but flagged off, and the best-named test suite (digest.quiet, attention.calm, pecr.classify). Regulatory gate by regulated_category with template replacement is clean. It is marked down on security because Google Cloud is a production sub-processor from day one, blobs rely on SSE-KMS plus presigned GETs, the share-sheet presigned PUT lands in the servable documents bucket before scanning, and a Supabase Storage path test is listed for storage that lives in S3. Escalating table fields to Flash-Lite contradicts the digest's 'stronger model for tables'. Its 10k cost under-counts its own lines.
GRAFTS:
  - [lean-founder] An execute-only Postgres role for the pipeline: security-definer functions in an intake schema (route_message, record_document, record_extraction), the worker's role holding no table grants, proved by a pgTAP case. :: Turns 'a compromised worker cannot read contracts' from an assertion into a test; it slots straight into security-first's core service as a second DB role.
  - [lean-founder] Build order: manual household on web with Simon's own data at week 4 (PITR on that day), ingestion afterwards; DPA signed and ZDR requested in week 0; Anthropic usage limit, Supabase spend cap and a CI test on per-document token budget. :: Real data reaches the store earliest with the smallest attack surface, and the cost-creep controls are cheap and named.
  - [lean-founder] Passwordless email one-time code as the primary factor with TOTP for aal2 actions, and a migration-author agent as the only agent permitted under supabase/migrations. :: No password database to breach or stuff; the migration guard makes the CVE-2025-48757 class unmergeable by construction rather than by review.
  - [product-first] Household model: people (members without logins), memberships.scope for property-limited viewers, contracts.supersedes_contract_id as the archive chain, attention_items with severity computing 'Nothing you need to do this month', notifications.classification enforced in the send path, and a junior role in the schema flagged off pending the Children's Code assessment. :: These are the multi-adult, multi-property and archive differentiators the competitor digest identifies; adding them later forces migrations.
  - [product-first] Confirm / Fix / Not ours cards with page evidence, one digest per member per morning, shared-email digest de-duplication, digest.quiet and attention.calm tests, and an eval workflow that re-runs the golden set across models. :: The calm promise becomes a tested property, and the cross-model eval gives the digest's comparison without a second production vendor.
  - [product-first] regulated_category derived on contracts selecting the prompt variant, a deterministic post-gate that replaces any verdict with a fixed template line, and a referrals table recording the disclosure shown and the FS Register check date. :: Makes the s21 FSMA line auditable per answer and per introduction.
  - [security-first] Stripping detected special-category data from extracted text before storage and answering the Article 9 question in the DPIA; CI-written 'last passing isolation test' and 'last restore drill' dates on the trust page; the erasure receipt naming the PITR and S3 version windows. :: Only security-first raised Article 9; the CI-stamped dates are the cheapest credible public evidence a one-person company can offer.
DISAGREEMENTS:
  - Where privileged code runs: security-first: one Fly.io container holds KMS and sb_secret, Edge Functions unused. lean-founder: two Lambdas plus Supabase Edge Functions, both privileged. product-first: Fly worker plus Edge Functions.
    VERDICT: One container as the single custody boundary (one place to log, one place to pentest), with lean's execute-only DB role for the pipeline; Edge Functions hold no secrets. Verify Fly's OIDC federation to AWS before falling back to IAM keys.
  - Document blobs: encryption and serving: security-first: encrypted under the household DEK before PUT, streamed only through core, no presigned URLs. lean-founder and product-first: SSE-KMS only, 5-minute presigned GETs after an RLS check.
    VERDICT: security-first. A leaked presigned URL is inert and erasure becomes DEK destruction, which covers S3 versions, cross-region replicas and backups at once - the erasure story that survives an ICO enquiry. Presigned PUT into a write-only intake prefix is acceptable for uploads.
  - Second extraction vendor on day one: product-first wires Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite into production per the digest; security-first and lean defer it.
    VERDICT: Build the adapter and run the eval workflow against redacted fixtures from day one; put no production data through Google until there is a reason. The digest wanted the comparison, not the sub-processor.
  - First penetration test: security-first and product-first: CREST web + API + mobile before public launch, Cobalt as interim. lean-founder: Cobalt web-only, CREST deferred to partner due diligence, 5,000 households or a paid store launch.
    VERDICT: Cobalt at private beta; CREST before premium goes on sale in the stores. Lean's own trigger fires at its phase 5 anyway, and a paid tier holding financial documents without a manual test of the apps and API is not defensible.
  - Free-tier access to the judgement model: security-first: 3 questions a month. lean-founder: 20 explain-this-document calls a month. product-first: premium only.
    VERDICT: Premium only for questions; free households get the deterministic attention list plus a fixed trial of three explain-this-document calls. The adviser is the FSMA risk surface and the cost-abuse surface; keep it small.
  - SES London fallback: security-first and lean: SES eu-west-1 Ireland. product-first: Cloudflare Email Routing to a Worker and R2 (eu).
    VERDICT: Ireland. It keeps the free spam and virus verdicts, adds no vendor, and is lawful under the adequacy decision renewed to December 2031.
  - Timeline to real households: lean: 10 weeks to 20-30 households, public beta Nov-Dec 2026. security-first and product-first: private beta Jan-Feb 2027, launch March 2027.
    VERDICT: Lean's ordering with security-first's gates; plan 12-16 weeks to 20-30 friendly households and March 2027 for public launch. The long poles are non-code: organisation developer accounts, SES production access, Cyber Essentials, and a hand-redacted golden set.
  - Primary authentication factor: security-first and product-first: email plus password with leaked-password protection. lean: passwordless email one-time code.
    VERDICT: Email one-time code plus TOTP for aal2, passkeys behind a flag with the RP ID fixed on day one. The intake path already treats the member's mailbox as the trust root, so a password adds a breach class without adding a root of trust.
  - Malware scanning host: security-first and product-first: clamd sidecar on Fly (4 GiB). lean: awslabs cdk-serverless-clamscan Lambda construct, Fly sidecar as fallback.
    VERDICT: The sidecar; it is the digest's recommendation and a known quantity. The construct only if its maintenance is verified at week 0.
  - Default document retention: security-first: live plus 7 years. product-first: live plus 6 years. lean: keep until deleted with a yearly prompt.
    VERDICT: Live plus 6 years, household-adjustable 1-10, stated in the notice. 'Keep until deleted' is the weakest answer to the storage-limitation principle.
  - Premium billing at launch: security-first and product-first say from day one; lean says phase 5 with gates in the schema from week 3.
    VERDICT: Not a real disagreement: all three take money only at public beta or launch. Gates in the first migration, RevenueCat and Stripe wired in sandbox during closed beta, on sale at launch with DMCC-shaped notices.
ERRORS FOUND:
  ! security-first: proposes the Batch API to halve extraction cost and lists it as a cost mitigation while claiming a zero-retention arrangement; the digest states the Batch API is not ZDR-eligible because batches are retained for 29 days. product-first has this right (batch not used).
  ! security-first: 'Fly has no native OIDC to AWS' is not in the digest and appears wrong (Fly Machines issue OIDC tokens usable for AWS IAM federation); the resulting design of long-lived IAM user keys in Fly secrets rotated quarterly is a weaker control than necessary. Verify before adopting.
  ! security-first and product-first: Supabase launch cost of USD 25 + 15 + 100 = 140 ignores the USD 10 compute credit included in Pro; the digest's figure is about USD 130. lean-founder nets it correctly.
  ! security-first: at 100k it adds 'Pro ... ~$550-600 plus Team $599'; Team replaces the Pro plan rather than stacking on it, so the base fee is double-counted (minor).
  ! lean-founder: the CREST deferral rule (first of partner due diligence, 5,000 households, or the store launch of a paid tier) is triggered by its own phase 5 (store submissions plus RevenueCat billing, Nov-Dec 2026), yet the cost table places CREST at 10,000 households.
  ! lean-founder: five friendly households forward real bills in phase 3 (weeks 5-7) but the privacy notice, trust page and complaints form are phase 4 deliverables (weeks 8-10) and the DPIA is only a draft; the regulatory digest requires the DPIA and transparency information before outside households' data is collected.
  ! lean-founder: 'citations cannot be combined with structured output' is not supported by the digest, which records only that PDF citations exist; treat as unverified.
  ! product-first: re-runs table fields and any field under 0.7 confidence on Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite; the digest's rule is 'a stronger model only when a document contains tables or the confidence field is low' and gives Haiku 4.5 the best cheap-tier table score (81.7) against 94-96 for the frontier tier, with no table score for Flash-Lite. The escalation target should be Sonnet 5 or Gemini 3.5 Flash.
  ! product-first: the 10k run cost of 'about GBP 700' under-counts its own listed lines; adding Intruder (USD 149-299) and SMS at GBP 124 plus VAT to the USD 684 subtotal gives roughly GBP 810-925.
  ! product-first: lists a storage_paths_scoped.sql pgTAP test for Supabase Storage object paths while storing every document in S3; the coherent assertion is security-first's 'storage.objects has no policies and is unused'.
  ! product-first: share-sheet uploads go by presigned PUT to hearth-documents/<household_id>/incoming/, the servable documents bucket, before scanning; the intake bucket is where unscanned bytes belong.
  ! product-first: states 'no other departures' from the research but, like the others, departs from the security digest's 'passkeys first, TOTP second' without listing it.
  ! product-first: 'DVLA VES closed to new registrations on 3 Sept 2026' misreads the digest, which says closed 'as of' 3 September 2026 (the date checked, not the closure date). Trivial.
  ! All three: 100k LLM lines differ by about 2x (security-first GBP 3,300-5,900; lean USD 2,300-4,500; product-first USD 2,500-4,000) on unmeasured scan ratios; the digest says to measure with count_tokens on real fixtures before publishing a per-household figure. Not an error, but none should be planned against.
SYNTHESIS:
Recommendation: security-first as the spine, lean-founder's build order and least-privilege plumbing, product-first's household model. Full text at scratchpad/panel/synthesis-judge-a.md.

Stack. Supabase Pro in eu-west-2 (created after 30 May 2026), Small compute, PITR on the day the first real household's data lands (about USD 130 a month net of the compute credit). One AWS account in eu-west-2: KMS (one customer-managed key, envelope encryption with EncryptionContext {household_id}, CloudTrail), SES receiving and sending, S3 (intake bucket, 7-day lifecycle; documents bucket, versioning, 14-day noncurrent expiry), SQS, CloudFront for the static Expo web export and the Astro marketing and trust site. One privileged service, core (Hono, TypeScript) on Fly.io lhr with a clamd sidecar: it alone holds kms:Decrypt and sb_secret (Fly OIDC federation to AWS if it verifies, else short-rotation keys) and reaches Postgres through two roles - an execute-only intake_worker calling security-definer intake.* functions with no table grants, proved by pgTAP, and the member's own JWT under RLS for anything member-facing. Edge Functions hold no secrets. Anthropic first-party API: Haiku 4.5 extraction, Sonnet 5 judgement, DPA signed and ZDR requested in week 0, Fable 5.1 never in the data path, no Batch API. Expo SDK 57, Expo Router, NativeWind, react-native-reusables; email one-time code plus TOTP (aal2 for decrypted fields, export, invites, rotation, deletion), passkeys behind a flag with the RP ID fixed on day one. Sentry EU without replay. RevenueCat and Stripe, DVSA MOT API, Ideal Postcodes from closed beta.

Data model. Security-first's tables with product-first grafted in: households (dek_wrapped, dek_version, intake token, retention); memberships with owner, adult, viewer and a junior role flagged off pending the Children's Code assessment; people for members without logins; viewer scope per property; properties with UPRN; vehicles; contracts with area, derived regulated_category and supersedes_contract_id; contract_history; documents; intake_messages; verified_senders and trusted_senders; extractions with model, prompt and schema versions and per-field evidence; reminder_rules; reminders; notifications with a PECR classification enforced in the send path; attention_items driving the dashboard header; marketing_consents; subscriptions with reminder_sent_at; assistant threads; observed_payments and bank_connections dark; append-only audit.events; erasure_requests and a household-free tombstone; ref.* public-read. Every domain table: household_id NOT NULL indexed, RLS FORCED, restrictive boundary plus four role-checked policies with WITH CHECK, anon revoked outside ref.*. _enc columns AES-256-GCM under the household DEK with AAD binding household, table, column and row. Blobs encrypted under the same DEK before PUT and streamed only by core after a JWT membership check, so erasure is DEK destruction plus deletes - provable across S3 versions, replicas and backups.

Ingestion. hh-<12-char token>@in.<domain> on SES London, fallback SES Ireland. Catch-all accept, silent drop for unknown tokens. Triage as a pure function over fixtures: virus FAIL dropped; DMARC FAIL from p=reject or p=quarantine dropped; verified sender with SPF or DKIM pass accepted; Gmail or Outlook ARC auto-forwards accepted; trusted provider domains accepted; everything else quarantined behind a 7-day 'was this you?' card; 60 a day per household and per-domain global limits. Parts: magic bytes (PDF, JPEG, PNG, HEIC; DOCX converted), clamd, pikepdf strip, re-encode, SHA-256. Uploads and scans use a presigned PUT into a write-only intake prefix and pass the same worker; a CI invariant asserts nothing lands in the documents prefix unscanned or unencrypted. Text-first extraction of pages 1-3, vision for scans, classify-then-select above 15 pages; code validates dates, sums and provider; the three email cases with the link never followed; Confirm, Fix or Not ours; two-level dedup.

AI tiering. Tier 0 code: totals, status, schedule, attention items, 'Nothing you need to do this month', a SavingsClaim type that cannot render without comparator, date, usage basis and exit fee. Tier 1 Haiku 4.5: no tools, one document, schema-only, injection corpus in CI; low-confidence and table fields escalate to Sonnet 5, not Flash-Lite. Tier 2 Sonnet 5 behind an explicit tap: premium at 30 a month, free households get the attention list plus three trial explain-this-document calls; one read-only tool under the member's JWT; regulated_category selects a describe-only prompt and a deterministic post-gate replaces any verdict with the template line; calls logged encrypted for 90 days. A Gemini adapter runs evals on redacted fixtures only; Google joins the sub-processor list only if it enters production.

Security and evidence. Day one: threat model and ASVS 5.0 L2, MASVS, GenAI LLM Top 10 2026 requirements imported into CLAUDE.md; pgTAP suite (rls_forced_everywhere, cross_household, grants, intake_worker_grants, audit_immutable, storage_unused), anon REST probe, bundle secret grep, envelope tests, an erasure test asserting zero rows, zero keys, a destroyed DEK and a tombstone; restore drill dated by CI; agents barred from pnpm add; incident plan and ICO fee before any outside household. Private beta: Cyber Essentials, Cobalt web test, privacy notice and DPIA live before the first friendly household. Before premium goes on sale: CREST web, API, iOS and Android. Trust page with CI-written isolation-test and restore-drill dates, sub-processor list, key custody, security.txt, no-NDA VDP. At 10k: ISO 27001, Supabase Team, log drain, private bounty, Open Banking agent registration.

Regulatory lines. Unregulated record, reminder and information product at MVP: no affiliate link or click reference until launch, then energy, broadband, mobile and boiler cover only, disclosed, price-ordered, behind separate per-channel deal consent, never in a reminder; insurance a plain unpaid link until an IAR; mortgages under Art 33A only with a broker agreement; nothing bank-derived until FCA agent registration; a test that no reminder template carries a partner link; DMCC-shaped premium; per-adult accounts with the invite as the Article 14 notice; Apple 5.1.2(i) consent in the forwarding onboarding; special-category text stripped and Article 9 answered in the DPIA.

Scope, phases, costs, build. Weeks 0-2 accounts (Digitelos organisation, SES production access, DPA and ZDR, ICO), schema, gates. Weeks 3-4 manual household with Simon's data, PITR on. Weeks 5-8 ingestion, 50-document golden set, abuse and injection suites, notice and DPIA live. Weeks 9-12 attention engine, Ofgem Annex 9 and TDCV, gated assistant, TestFlight and Play internal, Cyber Essentials, Cobalt, 20-30 households. Weeks 13-20 closed beta: properties, vehicles, DVSA, viewer scope, archive chain, export, ICS, billing in sandbox, accessibility pass, CREST. March 2027 public launch. H2 2027 Open Banking as agent. Run cost GBP 25 before real data, GBP 135-160 from the first household, GBP 200-230 at 1k, GBP 850-1,000 at 10k, GBP 6,000-9,000 at 100k with LLM measured on real fixtures; one-off before launch GBP 5,000-18,000. Fable 5.1 orchestrates in plan mode; Sonnet 5 implementer in a worktree; migration-author alone touches migrations; test-author before implementation; Fable security-reviewer on gated paths; Haiku fixture-maker; hooks deny .env, tfstate, pnpm add, db push and fly secrets; Stop runs check.sh; every PR passes typecheck, a11y lint, unit, pgTAP, anon probe, secret grep, Semgrep, gitleaks, Socket, frozen lockfile, golden-set floor, injection corpus, copy lint and axe; nightly rlsautotest, Maestro and EAS builds; a dated decision-log entry for anything a future session might undo.

JUDGE 2  winner: lean-founder
  security-first: total 52  sec 10 cost 6 speed 5 product 7 reg 8 build 8 evolve 8
    The strongest proof programme of the three: named pgTAP tests, a restrictive boundary policy, an erasure job that is itself a test, CI-stamped restore-drill and isolation dates on the trust page, an add-dep gate and an audit schema. One Hono container is the most agent-friendly server shape. But it is the slowest to real households (private beta January-February 2027, four months of building with no outside user), carries the highest launch bill and one-off spend, and adds bespoke code the digest did not ask for: application-level blob encryption streamed through the container, no presigned uploads, ARC-chain parsing. Its own MVP scope says native shells from month one while its phases put them in phase 3. It also claims a ZDR arrangement while budgeting the Batch API, which the digest says is not ZDR-eligible.
  lean-founder: total 56  sec 8 cost 9 speed 9 product 6 reg 9 build 8 evolve 7
    The only plan that reaches 20-30 real households inside ten weeks under GBP 150 a month and keeps the security floor the digest sets: forced RLS proven by pgTAP, envelope encryption, an intake_worker role with no table grants (the best single security idea in the panel), presign cross-household tests, a bundle grep and PITR from the first real data. Nothing arranges at MVP so the regulatory surface is minimal. Fewest vendors. Costs are the most carefully reconciled with the digest (USD 130 net for Supabase). Weaknesses: server logic is split across Deno Edge Functions, a Node Lambda and a container Lambda, which is harder for an agent to run locally than one process; vehicles are modelled as contract categories with a registration column on contracts; no people table; the product surface is thin (manual address, explain-only, DVSA deferred); cdk-serverless-clamscan is an unverified dependency outside the digest.
  product-first: total 54  sec 8 cost 7 speed 6 product 9 reg 8 build 7 evolve 9
    The best product thinking and data model: six moments drive the design, attention_items and a quiet-digest test make the calm promise testable, people without logins, viewer scope per property, supersedes chains, trusted_senders quarantine-and-confirm, notifications carrying a PECR classification, and named tests for every rule. It follows the digest most faithfully. Costs: it wires Gemini on Vertex from day one (a second DPA, sub-processor and GCP IAM before there is a reason, and its use for table re-runs is not evidenced), adds a Python sidecar for pikepdf to a TypeScript monorepo, ships Ideal Postcodes, DVSA, RevenueCat, Stripe, SMS and Cloudflare Pages inside the MVP, and puts public beta in January-February 2027. It also keeps a storage_paths_scoped.sql test that has nothing to test because documents live in S3.

ATTACK LENS: Regulatory and consumer-law sceptic: FCA perimeter (insurance and mortgage arranging and advising, IAR limits, AIS), Ofgem and Ofcom, UK GDPR and the DUAA (lawful basis for other household members and children, DPIA timing, transfers to the LLM vendor, erasure, the 19 June 2026 complaints duty), PECR, DMCC savings claims and the spring 2027 subscription regime, Apple and Google store rules, and the Equality Act - checked against both syntheses and the three proposals, grounded in the research digest.
  [HIGH] Boiler cover, breakdown and warranty affiliate links are insurance arranging, not an unregulated line
    Judge-a's regulatory lines (and security-first's commercial model) put boiler cover in the unregulated affiliate set with energy, broadband and mobile, priced at GBP 18-100 per plan from Hometree, YourRepair and Smart Cover. The digest says most boiler cover and breakdown are contracts of insurance (it lists 'insurance-based boiler cover / breakdown' under ICOBS and names Smart Cover as an FCA Appointed Representative paying '20% of first annual premium'). PERG 5.6 makes a paid, ongoing introduction relating to a contract of insurance Art 25(2) arranging with no Art 33 exclusion, and an unapproved promotion of a named insurer is a s21 FSMA breach - a criminal offence. The app cannot reliably tell a service plan from an insurance policy at the moment it renders a link.
    FIX: Remove boiler cover from the affiliate set until an IAR appointment exists. In the derived regulated_category, map boiler_cover, breakdown, home_emergency, warranty, service_plan and life_insurance to 'insurance' by default, overridable to 'none' only by a confirmed document statement that the product is not a contract of insurance. Make the per-area affiliate_links flag false for every area whose regulated_category is insurance, and add a copy-lint and policy test that no partner card or LLM answer can name a boiler cover, breakdown or warranty provider.
  [HIGH] People without logins (and children) have no lawful-basis or transparency route
    Both syntheses graft product-first's people table: members without logins carrying display_name, relationship, is_minor and date_of_birth_enc, with contracts, vehicles (keeper_person_id) and documents pointing at them. The Article 14 notice is 'the invite email', but these people are never invited, so the company processes named third parties' data (an elderly parent's policies, a teenager's phone contract, a child's date of birth) with no notice at all. The digest is explicit that the contract basis cannot cover another person's data, that legitimate interests needs an Article 14 notice within a month, and that under-18s' data needs Children's Code consideration and DUAA s81 'children's higher protection'. A DOB for a minor with no login is data the product does not need.
    FIX: Minimise: drop date_of_birth_enc for people without logins (keep an is_under_18 flag only if a feature needs it) and cap a person record at name, relationship and links to contracts. Add an optional contact on the person record and an Article 14 notice sent when one is supplied; where none is, the DPIA must record the Art 14(5)(b) reasoning and the terms must oblige the entering adult to tell the person. Treat Simon's own household members from week 3-4 the same way - the company is controller of their data too, so the notice, ICO fee and DPIA cannot wait for 'outside' households.
  [HIGH] Judge-b's phase plan collects friendly households' data before the DPIA, privacy notice and complaints procedure exist
    Judge-b's own error list faults lean-founder for having five friendly households forward real bills in weeks 5-7 while the DPIA and notice arrive in weeks 8-10, then repeats the fault: its phase 4 (weeks 8-10) has 5-10 friendly households live and its phase 5 (weeks 11-16) delivers 'trust page, DPIA'. The digest's launch checklist requires a DPIA before launch (AI extraction of financial documents combined across household members is screening-positive), Article 13/14 transparency before or at collection, the statutory complaints procedure in force since 19 June 2026, and the ICO fee before real data. Judge-a sequences this correctly (notice and DPIA live in weeks 5-8, households in weeks 9-12).
    FIX: Adopt judge-a's ordering as a gate, not a deliverable: no household other than Simon's own may join until the privacy notice (naming Anthropic, the 30-day retention pending ZDR, backup windows and retention defaults), the DPIA, the DUAA complaints form with 30-day acknowledgement, and the ICO registration are live. Write the gate into check.sh or the release checklist so an agent cannot ship an invite flow ahead of it.
  [HIGH] The fixture-maker sends unredacted real documents through the build tooling, which is not in the data-processing contracts
    All three proposals have a Haiku 4.5 fixture-maker agent that 'redacts real documents into fixtures' (Simon's own and, in product-first, friendly households' bills from Octopus, Admiral, Direct Line and others). Redaction by a model means the unredacted document is sent to Anthropic through Claude Code on a Claude Max subscription - consumer terms, not the API DPA, ZDR arrangement or SCCs the privacy notice describes - and the orchestrating Fable 5.1 session, which the digest says must 'never see production data', is in the loop. Household members' and friendly households' personal data would leave the contracted processing path.
    FIX: Redact deterministically before any model sees a document: a local script (pikepdf/regex over the text layer, manual for scans) produces the fixture, and only the redacted output reaches the fixture-maker. Obtain written agreement from any household whose documents become fixtures, keep fixtures free of anything that could identify them, and record in the DPIA that build tooling never receives live personal data. Extend the same rule to pentesters: Cobalt and CREST test a staging stack seeded with synthetic data, or sign a processor contract.
  [MEDIUM] 'Three opportunities to save in the next 90 days' is a savings claim without a figure, and it counts insurance and mortgage renewals
    The brief's dashboard line survives in every proposal and both syntheses as 'a count of switchable contracts ending within 90 days with no figure'. Under DMCC s226 and CMA207 a claim about 'the existence of a specific price advantage' is a misleading action if unsupported, and for home and motor insurance the digest shows the hook is structurally weak because ICOBS 6B forbids renewal prices above the new-business price. Labelling an insurance renewal or a mortgage fix expiry as an 'opportunity to save' next to a comparison-site link also supplies the 'narrative encouraging the click' that PERG 8.4.11 says turns a bare link into an inducement.
    FIX: Rename the header to a factual count - 'three contracts to review in the next 90 days' or 'ending in the next 90 days' - and reserve 'save' for a rendered SavingsClaim (comparator, date, usage basis, exit fees, VAT flag) which today can exist only for energy against the Ofgem cap. Keep insurance and mortgage items in the review list with the ICOBS 'shop around' wording and context figures only, never under a 'save' heading. Add this to attention.opportunities.test.ts.

    Both syntheses say deal suggestions go behind a separate per-channel consent 'not the soft opt-in for free users', implying premium subscribers could be marketed to under reg 22(3). The soft opt-in covers the sender's own 'similar products or services'; an energy supplier's tariff is not the app's similar service, and the digest flags even the free-tier case as an open question. Separately, the PECR rule is encoded on reminder templates, but product-first's morning digest also carries attention items (price_rise, renewal_window, out_of_contract); once affiliates exist an item with a partner link inside a digest is direct marketing with fines now up to GBP 17.5m or 4% of turnover. The digest also leaves open whether push counts as 'electronic mail'.
    FIX: State that deal suggestions rest on explicit per-channel consent only, for every tier, with unsubscribe honoured on push, email and SMS alike, and treat push as electronic mail. Move classification from template id to message content: pecr-classify runs over the assembled digest and any attention item, refuses marketing without consent, and a test asserts a digest containing a partner link is classified marketing. Ensure List-Unsubscribe on service email cannot suppress the DMCC statutory renewal notices.

The digest's NCSC rule (privilege drops to the sender's) has been applied to the LLM and not to the parsers, which are the older and better-exploited surface (libheif, LibreOffice and qpdf all have CVE histories). WHAT SURVIVES: The architecture itself holds up under this lens and I could not make it cheaper without making it weaker. Supabase Pro in London with Small compute and PITR at about USD 130 is correctly priced against the digest and is the right floor; moving documents to S3 with versioning is cheaper to operate than Supabase Storage plus a replication job and it is the one part of the backup story that is genuinely self-healing; envelope encryption under one KMS key costs a dollar and the dek_version re-wrap doubles as the exit path; one Hono service on Fly with a clamd sidecar is the smallest thing that can run under the Stop hook and hold the secrets, and the intake_worker execute-only role is a control that costs a migration. Haiku 4.5 plus Sonnet 5 with Fable 5.1 out of the data path and the Batch API rejected is right on both cost and retention; extraction really is a rounding error at launch and at 1,000 households, and the 10k LLM figure is within range. Reminders as rows in Postgres with pg_cron and pgmq beat every orchestration vendor at every scale in the digest, push-first with SMS premium-only is the only affordable ladder, and the eight-vendor launch list with a named runner-up (Neon plus Hono plus Better Auth) is as portable as a managed stack gets. The build order - manual household with Simon's own data first, PITR that day, ingestion second - puts real data behind the smallest surface earliest. No Open Banking in the MVP and premium rather than commissions are both forced by the evidence. The pgTAP isolation kit, the anon probe, the secret grep and the CI-stamped trust-page dates are the cheapest demonstrable security a one-person company can offer and they survive every failure mode above. Cyber Essentials early, Cobalt at closed beta and the DMCC-shaped premium tier are right; only CREST's timing and scope needed rebalancing.
VERDICT: Build it, but fix the operations story before the first outside household, not at 10k. The syntheses are sound on shape and 15-25% optimistic on money: the honest launch figure is about GBP 165 a month of run cost plus GBP 100-200 of Claude tooling and 20% VAT while unregistered, about GBP 245 at 1,000, GBP 1,100 at 10,000 (GBP 1,580 with Team) and GBP 8,150 at 100,000 before a single salary, against net premium revenue nearer GBP 52-57k than GBP 80k at that scale. Three holes cost pence and a day each and must be phase 1-2 deliverables: an off-platform nightly dump in a second AWS account with KMS deletion alarms and a named deputy (the 7-day PITR window is shorter than a fortnight's illness and the wrapped DEKs live only in Postgres); a liveness gate on 'Nothing you need to do this month' plus a dead-man's switch, 14-day SQS retention and a 30-day intake lifecycle (silence must never be a failure mode in a product that sells calm); and SES treated as production from day one with bounce and complaint alarms, separate sending identities, a canary mailbox and passkeys promoted to an email-independent first factor (passwordless-only login on a new domain makes one mail pause a total lockout). Beyond those, the price cliffs are dated and knowable - EAS Update per MAU, RevenueCat at USD 2.5k, VAT registration around 1,900 premium households, Apple's 30% above USD 1m, Supabase Team when a principal asks, Haiku 4.5's retirement with a 1.3x tokenizer step - and belong in the decision log with their triggers. Phase CREST by what is stable (web and API before premium, mobile three months after store launch) to keep pre-revenue one-offs near GBP 10-12k. Verify in week 0: Fly OIDC to AWS, PITR restore semantics, Supabase spend-cap and auth-mailer behaviour, SES receiving in London, and the company's VAT position.

ATTACK LENS: Agent-buildability sceptic. I have read the full research digest (all nine sections plus the critic), the Claude Code build notes, both syntheses and the flow-watch scaffolding they cite. I accept the architecture the judges converged on (Supabase Pro London with forced RLS and a restrictive boundary, one Hono service on Fly as the sole privileged runtime, SES into S3, Haiku 4.5 with no tools, Sonnet 5 behind a tap, Fable 5.1 out of the data path, Expo SDK 57, email OTP plus TOTP, PITR from the first real household). My question is narrower: given Sonnet 5 writes about 90% of this, where does the plan rely on an agent remembering something rather than on a gate that fails? Every finding below names the failure I expect, the evidence for expecting it, and the file, hook or test that turns it into a red CI run instead of a production incident. Where I depart from a judge or the digest I say so.
  [CRITICAL] The implementer will weaken the gates rather than satisfy them - nothing in the plan stops it
    The syntheses give the implementer Edit/Write plus Bash for pnpm test and supabase test db, and make check.sh the Stop hook. An agent that cannot finish because a test fails has three cheaper moves than fixing the code: mark the test .skip or .only, lower the golden-set floor in the eval config, or edit check.sh, the pgTAP helper, or the hook itself. flow-watch survives this because Simon reads every diff, but here the volume is ten times higher and the test-author is a separate agent whose work the implementer can overwrite in the same worktree. The digest's evidence on AI-built code (45% OWASP flaw rate, D-grade Firebase rules, C-grade Supabase scope checks) is about code; the failure here is about the agent editing the thing that judges the code. No judge names this.
    FIX: Three mechanical controls, none of them conventions. (1) Per-agent hooks in .claude/agents/implementer.md: a PreToolUse guard (.claude/hooks/pre-tool-guard.sh) that denies Edit/Write under supabase/tests/**, **/*.test.ts, **/*.eval.ts, fixtures/**, packages/policy/**, prompts/**, .claude/**, .github/**, check*.sh, deps-allowlist.json, supabase/migrations/** and infra/** for that agent, whatever the branch. Only test-author may touch tests, only migration-author may touch migrations, and .claude/**, .github/** and check*.sh are Simon-only via CODEOWNERS with required review. (2) A spec scope fence: every docs/specs/NN-*.md carries YAML frontmatter `may_edit:` globs; the guard reads the spec named in the branch (spec/NN-*) and denies edits outside those globs, so an implementer cannot wander into vault/ to 'fix' something. (3) scripts/ci/no-skipped-tests.sh fails CI on `.skip(`, `.only(`, `xit(`, `xdescribe(`, `it.todo(` and pgTAP `skip(` or `todo(` anywhere; the golden-set floor, injection corpus list and eval thresholds live in packages/policy/gates.json, which is in the protected set. Add a fourth: a gate self-test job in ci.yml that checks out fixtures/gate-canaries/ (a migration without RLS, a bundle with sb_secret_, a skipped test, a prompt edit without a version bump, a schema with `minimum`) and asserts each gate goes red. A gate that has never fired is an assumption, and this job runs on every change to .claude/**, .github/** or scripts/ci/**.
  [CRITICAL] RLS will be bypassed through views, RPC functions and the memberships table, not through the policies the pgTAP suite checks
    The June 2026 benchmark failure the digest cites is policies that check identity but not scope, and the restrictive household_boundary policy plus 001_rls_forced_everywhere and 010_cross_household answer that exactly. But Sonnet 5 reaches for three things those tests do not see. Views: an agent building the dashboard will create v_dashboard_rows for convenience; a view owned by postgres runs with the owner's privileges and ignores the base tables' RLS unless security_invoker is set, and PostgREST will happily expose it. Functions: a `get_household_contracts(household_id uuid)` helper written SECURITY DEFINER for speed and left in public is callable by any authenticated user through /rpc with any household_id. Memberships: private.household_ids() reads memberships as definer, so memberships itself needs policies, and the obvious one (`user_id = auth.uid()`) means a member cannot see who else is in their household, so the agent widens it to `household_id in (select private.household_ids())` - correct - or, if it hits the recursion warning, to using(true).
