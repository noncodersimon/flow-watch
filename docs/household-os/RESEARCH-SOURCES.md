# Household OS - research sources

Companion to ARCHITECTURE.md. Eleven research sweeps were run on 3 September 2026 by web-enabled agents; each finding below carries the source it was read from and the confidence the agent assigned. Prices and policies change - re-check anything load-bearing before relying on it.

## UK competitor landscape for household bill, contract and document management, September 2026

**Recommendation.** Build the combination nobody offers, and do not bet the business model on affiliate commissions. Position the product as the household's shared memory and calm administrator, not as a switching service: (1) ingest by forwarding address and upload first (Orlo and Tidee prove the pattern works and are cheap to build), add Open Banking later for reconciliation and price-rise detection rather than as the entry point, because Snoop and Emma already own the pure-banking angle and each connected user costs money; (2) make non-money records first-class from day one - MOT, warranties, boiler cover, TV licence, council tax, mortgage fix expiry - which is where Onedox's users found the most value and where Nous, Snoop, Emma and Taupia are absent; (3) treat household as multi-adult with roles and multiple properties (only UtilityPilot, Orlo workspaces and Switchd handle more than one address), since two-person sharing is already table stakes across Roost, HomeVaultHQ, Hattie, Tidee and Nous; (4) make the archive a feature (Orlo's Year in Review and HomeVaultHQ's Home Passport are the only attempts) and let the frontier model answer 'what changed and what should we do' questions rather than ext

**Contested points.**
- Alfred is usually filed alongside Nous and Snoop, but it is a regulated wholesale provider charging £195/year, competing with Utility Warehouse; it holds no documents and tracks no renewals, so it is a potential partner or switching destination rather than a rival organiser.
- Nous's own pages disagree on price: the pricing page and homepage say Premium is £6.99/month, while the how-it-works page says £7.99/month with 'no charge until we have saved you £100'. Treat the pricing page as current and the other as stale copy.
- Nous user numbers are quoted three ways - 'more than 100,000 households' on the homepage, 'over 375k households' on about-us and in the July 2026 funding note - which most likely separates paying or managed households from registrations; neither figure is audited.
- 'Household sharing' is not the unoccupied ground it is often assumed to be: Nous Premium (unlimited logins), Roost, HomeVaultHQ (5 seats), Hattie Plus (10), Orlo Pro (10), Tidee (5) and UtilityPilot Household (5) all ship it. The real gaps are multi-property, non-money records combined with banking data, and long-lived archive plus assistant.
- The space looks crowded on a feature checklist but is thin on scale: apart from Nous, Snoop and Emma, every organiser found (Hattie 1 rating, Taupia ~500 households, Orlo, Roost, HomeVaultHQ, Tidee, Remindwise, Nevr, Stead) launched between late 2025 and August 2026 and appears to be a solo or three-person team with no announced funding.
- Auto-switching is often still described as a live category; in practice Flipper (2021) and Look After My Bills are gone, Switchd is the lone survivor, and Taupia's ~£20-per-fuel commission shows how little a switch pays. Nous's own copy says it does not rely on commissions.
- Moneyhub's consumer closure is reported with different dates (early 2025, 31 July, 14 August 2026); the reliable facts are the 14 February 2025 withdrawal announcement, an 18-month wind-down and the handover to WPS Advisory from July 2025.
- Homelyfe is listed in some competitor sets but was an insurtech that stopped quoting in 2022; HomeVault is three unrelated products sharing a name (HomeVaultHQ UK, homevault.uk UK, and US inventory apps).
- Plum's bill switching is repeatedly cited in reviews but Plum's current pricing and fee pages are built entirely around savings and investing; assume it is vestigial. Chip has never done bills.
- Third-party claims of 'AI-trained on UK contract formats' (Household Copilot) and 'syncs directly with UK providers' (SimpliHome) could not be verified against a primary source and should be treated as marketing until tested.

## How a small UK startup gets Open Banking account data (AIS) legally and cheaply in 2026 - regulatory routes, provider terms, consent rules, smart data outlook, and the recommended phase for a household operating system

**Recommendation.** Keep Open Banking out of the MVP and plan it as phase 2. The reasoning: (1) the moment the app shows bank-derived bills to a household it is providing AIS, so it needs either an FCA agent registration under an aggregator (principal approval, up to 2 months at the FCA, forced co-branding, and with Plaid a contractual obligation to get your own registration) or its own RAISP registration (£1,130 fee, mandatory PII at roughly EUR 5-30k a year, a compliance function, wind-down plan and 4-6 months); (2) there is no longer a free production tier for UK account data - every credible provider is sales-led, per-connected-user, usually with minimum commitments - so the cost is real and the negotiation is slow; (3) the 90-day reconfirmation cycle is a retention tax that needs designed UX, not an afterthought; (4) HMT's July 2026 consultation and the Q4 2026 open banking SI may change access pricing and SCA rules within a year, so signing a multi-year aggregator contract now is premature; and (5) the product's core value (contracts, renewal dates, documents, provider details) comes from forwarded emails, uploads and manual entry, which need no FCA permission and already cover the switching-com

**Contested points.**
- The 'TPP on behalf' model where the aggregator is the regulated party and the startup stays unregulated does NOT cover this product. PERG 15.3 Q25A and Yapily's own guidance are explicit: if you show users more than name, account number and sort code (e.g. a bill or transaction derived from their account), you must be a registered agent or hold your own registration. Several 2026 comparison sites imply otherwise.
- GoCardless Bank Account Data / Nordigen is still widely listed in 2026 as the cheapest UK AIS option with a free 50-connection tier. It is closed to new signups; anything recommending it is stale.
- Openbankingtracker describes TrueLayer and Yapily as 'free sandbox, pay-as-you-go production'. Their own sites contradict this: TrueLayer has removed its pricing page and Yapily's says 'tailored pricing, talk to an expert'. Assume sales negotiation and minimum commitments.
- The 90-day rule was not 'scrapped' (as PYMNTS and Fintech Times headlines from 2021 say). It moved from bank SCA to AISP reconfirmation of consent, and it still terminates access if the user does not respond. Retention impact is smaller (one tap versus re-authenticating at each bank) but non-zero.
- Consultants variously quote the RAISP application fee as £1,000 or £1,500; the FCA's current page shows Category 3 = £1,130, and the 2026/27 fee instrument governs. Application fees are a minor part of the true cost (PII, compliance officer, legal review).
- The agent model is often sold as 'no regulation'. In practice the FCA expects the principal to assess your directors' fitness and propriety, you carry the principal's branding and disclaimers, the principal can be selective about small agents, and Plaid contractually requires eventual own registration.
- Plaid's pricing page says free Limited Production allows 200 live API calls per product; an independent tracker says newer accounts get roughly 10 live Items. Either way it is a test allowance, not a free tier.
- Smart data is frequently presented as imminent for utilities. The government's own documents give autumn 2026 for a consultation, 2027/28 for energy regulations, November 2027 for consumer-specific tariff sharing, and no commitment at all for telecoms.

**Findings.**
- Showing consolidated bank-account information to the user is AIS; the firm that displays it (directly or via an agent) is the one that must be registered or authorised, and processing the data first does not take it outside the perimeter. [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg15/perg15s3
- Own RAISP registration: FCA application fee is Category 3 (£1,130), no capital requirement, but professional indemnity insurance or a comparable guarantee is mandatory, plus a business plan, governance, wind-down plan, incident reporting, sensitive-data and security policies, and registration of every director and manager. [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/apply/fees
- Statutory timeline: the FCA must decide within 3 months of a complete application and within 12 months of any application; the clock only starts when the FCA confirms completeness, so real-world RAISP timelines run 4-6 months. [high] - https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/752/regulation/9
- PII for a RAISP is sized by the EBA formula; a new AIS-only firm with no track record is floored at EUR 50,000 of cover on the size criterion, plus EUR 50,000 more if the firm also carries on non-payment business (which a household app does). Market premiums for seed-stage AISP policies are roughly EUR 5,000-30,000 a year for EUR 250k-1m of cover. [medium] - https://www.eba.europa.eu/documents/10180/1901998/6411f24d-e430-4e05-ab03-1393a3f865cb/Final%20Guidelines%20on%20PII%20under%20PSD2%20(EBA-GL-2017-08).pdf
- Agent route: the principal AISP registers you via FCA Connect; the FCA has up to 2 months; you cannot start until you appear on the register; the principal is liable for everything you do, must vet your directors' fitness and propriety, must hold PII covering its agents, and the AIS contract is between the principal and the end user. The FCA charge to the principal is £3 per agent. [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/agency-models-under-psd2
- Agent status is not licence-free operation: TrueLayer requires its logo and name on the bank-selection dialog, bars white-labelling, and requires a disclaimer naming TrueLayer (FRN 901096) wherever balances or transactions are shown; Plaid limits its agency model to customers on its 'Scale' plan and requires agents to become registered or authorised themselves within predefined timeframes; Yapily says sharing consolidated data with UK users without being on the register as its agent 'is illegal'. [high] - https://docs.yapily.com/pages/tools-and-services/yapilyconnect/yapilyconnect-ux-ais-guidelines/
- The 90-day rule did not disappear; it moved. Since 26 March 2022 banks may apply the Article 10A exemption and skip SCA, but the AISP must reconfirm the customer's explicit consent at least every 90 days (Article 36(6)), stop access if they do not, and SCA at the bank is still needed to fetch more than 90 days of transactions. This is unchanged as of September 2026; the EEA is at 180 days with SCA. [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps21-19.pdf
- The regulatory framework is about to move: HM Treasury's 'Modernising Payment Services Regulation' consultation (14 July 2026, closes 6 October 2026) commits to revoking the PSRs' SCA requirements so the FCA can set outcome-focused rules, gives the FCA powers over the Future Entity replacing Open Banking Limited, and consults on letting banks charge third parties for API access that is free today (with protection for fintechs relying on free access). [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/modernising-payment-services-regulation/modernising-payment-services-regulation-consultation
- GoCardless Bank Account Data (formerly Nordigen), the only meaningful free UK/EU AIS tier, is closed to new signups; existing accounts continue. It is not an option for a new product. [high] - https://bankaccountdata.gocardless.com/new-signups-disabled
- TrueLayer: production pricing is sales-led with no public price list; the sandbox is free; it offers an agent model; data is stored primarily in the UK and EEA; transactions carry a beta classification and merchant_name for UK, Irish and French banks; Data API v3 is UK-only and includes a consent-reconfirmation endpoint. [high] - https://docs.truelayer.com/docs/enable-your-users-to-connect-their-bank-account
- Plaid (UK): Plaid Financial Ltd is FCA-authorised (FRN 804718); UK production pricing is sales-led (the published pay-as-you-go card-billed plan is US/Canada); the sandbox is free and 'Limited Production' allows up to 200 live API calls per product; Recurring Transactions is available in the UK as an add-on; but EEA/UK data is transferred to and stored in AWS regions in the United States. [high] - https://plaid.com/legal/
- Yapily: Yapily Connect Ltd is FCA-authorised for AIS and PIS (FRN 827001); unregulated customers use 'delegated registration' under Yapily Connect, but must be FCA-registered agents to show consolidated data to UK users; pricing is 'tailored' with no published tiers; the sandbox is free; hosting is Google Cloud London (primary) and Belgium (secondary) plus AWS Frankfurt, with transaction enrichment sub-processed by Ntropy (US). [high] - https://www.yapily.com/legal/end-user-terms
- Moneyhub: FCA-authorised AISP/PISP (FRN 809360); API clients providing AIS are appointed as Moneyhub agents; data is processed and stored in the UK and EEA; the API has a built-in regular-transaction detector (frequency, predicted next date and amount, gaps, returned payments) that needs as few as 3 monthly transactions; pricing is sales-led with a developer sandbox. [high] - https://docs.moneyhubenterprise.com/docs/regular-transaction-series
- Tink, Salt Edge, Bud and Finexer round out the UK options: Tink Financial Services Ltd (FRN 988456) shows prices only to existing customers and its Standard tier runs under Tink's licence; Salt Edge runs a Partner Program for firms without a licence (free sandbox, 'Test' status limited to 100 live connections for 90 days, then per-consented-user pricing on quote); Bud Financial Ltd (FRN 793327) is enterprise-only with no public pricing but strong enrichment including a 'Regularity' recurring-payment signal; Finexer (FRN 925695, UK-only AIS+PIS) advertises fixed monthly Startup/Standard/Enterprise plans with no setup fees and a free sandbox but publishes no figures. [medium] - https://tink.com/pricing/
- Every major provider offers a free sandbox with mock or simulated banks, so the integration, consent flow and 90-day reconfirmation logic can be built and tested at zero cost before any commercial or regulatory commitment. [high] - https://www.openbankingtracker.com/guides/free-open-banking-apis
- Smart data under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 will not deliver utility or telecoms account data in time for an MVP: Part 1 powers commenced at Royal Assent (19 June 2025), the Smart Data 2035 strategy (26 March 2026) targets 5+ schemes by 2030 and 20+ by 2035 with £36m over four years, the open banking SI is expected Q4 2026, DESNZ will consult on a detailed energy scheme in autumn 2026 aiming for regulations in 2027/28, and telecoms has no committed scheme ('further consideration, including the merits of a voluntary industry-led scheme'). [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/smart-data-strategy
- The nearest concrete energy data hook is Tariff Interoperability: suppliers must expose public tariff pricing data via APIs from 18 February 2027 (Phase 1a), and consumer-consented sharing of customer-specific tariff information with authorised third parties is planned for November 2027 (Phase 1b), aligned with RECCo's Consumer Consent Solution. [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/tariff-interoperability
- The FCA's Open Finance Roadmap (published 13-14 April 2026) is a 2026-2030 programme: a discussion paper on a first open finance scheme in Q4 2026, options for a long-term framework in 2027, and scheme launches only in 2028-2030, prioritising SME lending and mortgages - not household bills. [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/corporate-documents/open-finance-roadmap

## Regulatory perimeter for a UK household bills app in 2026 - what it may do without authorisation, and what needs it

**Recommendation.** Ship the MVP as an unregulated 'household record, reminder and information' product and draw three explicit lines in the copy and the code.

**Contested points.**
- 'Linking to a comparison site is harmless' is only true for insurance if the link is unpaid, generic and not part of an ongoing arrangement (PERG 5.6.18); a commission-paid link to a named insurer or PCW is Art 25(2) arranging and probably a s21 promotion. For energy and telecoms the same link is unregulated.
- 'You would lose X cover by switching' is not automatically advice: a factual feature comparison is information under PERG 5.8.10, but the FCA says it 'is likely to take on the nature of advice if the circumstances ... give it the force of a recommendation'. The line is the verdict, not the data, and an LLM will drift over it unless constrained.
- The Consumer Duty is often described as reaching unregulated introducers. The FCA's own page says only firms conducting regulated activities are subject to it; the practical effect arrives through partners' contracts, not the rulebook.
- Many summaries still say the DMCC subscription rules commence in 2026 (the Ashurst note says 'Spring 2026'). The government's April 2026 response and ministerial statement moved commencement to spring 2027. The £400m figure is genuine but is 'consumer benefit' modelled from £14 a month per unwanted subscription, not measured savings.
- The Data (Use and Access) Act is sometimes presented as creating a broad 'recognised legitimate interests' basis that eases commercial processing. Schedule 4 lists public-interest purposes only (national security, crime, emergencies, safeguarding, public tasks); a bills app still needs the ordinary three-part legitimate interests test.
- Energy comparison sites are widely assumed to be 'Ofgem regulated'. Ofgem states it has no direct role regulating TPIs through licence; the Confidence Code is voluntary and its whole-of-market rule was removed in 2018. Statutory regulation was confirmed in October 2025 but still needs primary legislation.
- Vendor marketing and some compliance blogs assert that the UK-US Data Bridge covers transfers to US AI vendors. OpenAI has no DPF entry (June 2026 review) and Anthropic's status is contradicted between a June 2026 law-firm note (not certified) and a May 2026 vendor guide (participates); both vendors' DPAs rest on SCCs plus the UK Addendum instead.
- Mortgage referral is not, as sometimes claimed, a regulated activity for the introducer: Art 33A explicitly excludes commission-paid introductions to authorised brokers subject to disclosure and no borrower money. What is regulated is a product-level or switch-now recommendation.
- Ofcom comparison-tool accreditation fees quoted in law-firm summaries (£11,500 initial, £7,000 re-accreditation, reduced for small or not-for-profit tools) were not confirmed on a live Ofcom page during this research.

**Findings.**
- Insurance advice is regulated only when it is a recommendation about a particular contract; factual comparison is information, but presenting it selectively or with a verdict turns it into advice [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5/perg5s8
- A paid, ongoing insurance referral is 'arranging' and the general introducing exclusion does not apply to insurance; an unauthorised app taking commission on insurance leads needs authorisation or appointed-representative status [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5/perg5s6
- FCA guidance on comparison websites says affiliate links, pre-purchase filtering and star ratings are arranging and probably advice, and that unauthorised sites promoting insurance risk a criminal s21 breach [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg11_17.pdf
- The Introducer Appointed Representative route lets an unauthorised app make introductions to one authorised principal and distribute that principal's approved non-real-time promotions, and nothing more [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/principals-appointed-representatives/responsibilities-how-to-oversee
- Price comparison websites hold FCA authorisation for insurance distribution (or are appointed representatives of a group firm that does) and state that their energy, MOT and telecoms switching are not FCA-regulated [high] - https://www.comparethemarket.com/about-us/
- Full FCA authorisation as an insurance or mortgage intermediary costs a £2,820 application fee, minimum capital of £5,000 or 2.5% of income, and professional indemnity cover of at least EUR 1.3m per claim [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/apply/fees
- Showing a mortgage fixed-rate expiry and pointing to a broker is not regulated advice; recommending a particular product or telling the borrower to switch from variable to fixed is [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg4/perg4s6
- Mortgage broker referrals with commission are available to an unauthorised app under the Art 33A introducing exclusion, subject to no money from the borrower and up-front disclosure of the commission [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg4/perg4s5
- Any in-app promotion of a named insurer, broker or mortgage product is a financial promotion needing approval by an authorised firm; the generic and introduction exemptions do not fit an affiliate model [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8/perg8s12
- The Consumer Duty does not bind an unauthorised app directly, but every regulated partner must assess the app as part of its distribution chain and will contract Duty-style obligations onto it [high] - https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/consumer-duty-information-firms
- Energy comparison, auto-switching and referral services are not regulated by Ofgem today; the Confidence Code is a voluntary accreditation with independence, commission-disclosure and price-ordering rules [high] - https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/decision/confidence-code-code-practice-online-domestic-price-comparison-services
- Government confirmed on 23 October 2025 that Ofgem will regulate energy third-party intermediaries, including auto-switchers, bill-splitters and PCWs, once legislation is passed; sales-lead generators are outside the initial scope [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/regulating-third-party-intermediaries-tpis-in-the-retail-energy-market/outcome/regulating-third-party-intermediaries-tpis-in-the-retail-energy-market-government-response-accessible-webpage
- Ofcom requires telecoms providers to send end-of-contract notifications 10 to 40 days before the minimum term ends and annual best-tariff notifications thereafter; comparison tools are not Ofcom-regulated but can join a voluntary accreditation scheme [high] - https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-2-6-weeks/130197-helping-consumers-get-better-deals-on-their-broadband/ecn-consultation-and-statement/statement-helping-consumers-get-better-deals.pdf?v=323929
- The ICO data protection fee is £52 (Tier 1), £78 (Tier 2) or £3,763 (Tier 3), with a £5 direct debit discount; a start-up with under £632,000 turnover or 10 staff pays Tier 1 [high] - https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/
- A DPIA is required before launch: AI extraction of financial documents, combining data from several sources and household members, and any Open Banking feed each hit ICO high-risk triggers [high] - https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/when-do-we-need-to-do-a-dpia/
- The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025's main data provisions commenced on 5 February 2026 and the statutory complaints procedure on 19 June 2026; 'recognised legitimate interests' are public-interest only and do not help a commercial app [high] - https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/regulation/2/made
- Data about a household member who is not the account holder cannot rest on the contract basis; legitimate interests with an Article 14 notice within a month is the working basis, and under-18s' data needs Children's Code consideration [high] - https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/when-should-we-provide-privacy-information/
- Sending household data to a US LLM API is a restricted transfer; the UK-US Data Bridge only works for vendors on the DPF list with the UK Extension, which OpenAI is not and Anthropic may not be, so plan on SCCs plus the UK Addendum and a transfer risk assessment [medium] - https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/adequacy-regulations/how-does-the-uk-extension-to-the-eu-us-data-privacy-framework-work/
- The DMCC Act subscription regime is now expected to commence in spring 2027, and the government's estimate of about £400m a year of consumer benefit is verified [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime/outcome/government-response-to-consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime-web-accessible-version

## LLM economics, data residency and open-weight options for structured extraction from household documents, September 2026

**Recommendation.** Start with Claude Haiku 4.5 as the extraction model behind a provider-neutral extraction interface, with Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite on Vertex EU multi-region wired up as the second implementation from day one and used for the eval comparison; use Sonnet 5 (ZDR-eligible, USD 0.026 per question) for adviser questions and keep Fable 5.1 out of any path that sees personal documents until Anthropic's Enterprise Frontier Safeguards makes it available without 30-day retention. Make the claim you can prove instead - documents are never used to train any AI model, are processed under a written zero-retention or 30-day-maximum retention arrangement, are held in the UK or EU at rest under your own keys, and are deleted on request - and route by sensitivity: text-first extraction from email bodies and PDF text layers (cheap, few tokens), vision only for scans, and a stronger model only when a document contains tables 

**Contested points.**

## What "secure from the outset and demonstrably so" means in 2026 for a small UK consumer app holding households' bills, policies and documents: assurance schemes and costs, pentesting, engineering standards, concrete controls, trust pages, AI-agent build risks, and a costed launch checklist

**Recommendation.** Treat "demonstrably secure" as three artefacts that cost almost nothing and one that costs real money, and sequence them.

**Contested points.**
- The brief asks about the 'Willow' question set: Willow (v3.2) was superseded by 'Danzell' (Requirements v3.3) for every Cyber Essentials account created from 27 April 2026, with automatic failure for any cloud service lacking MFA and for high/critical patches older than 14 days; Willow-based certificates can only still be issued to accounts opened before that date.
- Cyber Essentials is not evidence that the app is secure: the NCSC requirements put 'bespoke and custom components of web applications' out of scope and point to ASVS / the Software Security Code of Practice instead. It certifies the company's devices, cloud accounts and patching, which is still worth £320 for the MFA discipline and the badge.
- The brief cites the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025; the current edition is the OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026, published 4 August 2026 (Excessive Agency is now LLM03, 'Hidden Context Exposure' replaces System Prompt Leakage). Cite 2026 in the threat model.
- MASVS 'levels' no longer exist in the standard: v2 removed L1/L2/R and moved them to MAS Testing Profiles in MASWE/MASTG. Pentest vendors still quote 'MASVS-L1/L2', which is fine as scoping language but not as a certification claim.
- SOC 2 via Vanta/Drata/Secureframe is largely irrelevant for a UK consumer app: none of the five UK fintech trust pages reviewed cite it, UK partners cite ISO 27001 and Cyber Essentials, and the all-in first-year cost (platform $14k+ plus audit $10k-$50k) buys nothing a UK household or Open Banking principal asks for.
- 'Bank-grade encryption' and 'military-grade AES-256' (Nous, Snoop, Emma) are marketing phrases; a credible trust page names the certificate, the tester, the date, the key custodian and the sub-processors, as Moneyhub's does. Copy the specificity, not the slogans.
- Prompt injection cannot be solved by filtering: NCSC explicitly warns that deny-listing phrases like 'ignore previous instructions' is ineffective and that vendors claiming to 'stop' injection should be treated sceptically. Products marketed as prompt-injection firewalls reduce likelihood at best; the architecture (no tools for the extractor, privilege drops to the sender's) is the control.
- Zero data retention with Anthropic is not available for Covered Models including Claude Fable 5.1 and Fable 5; if the orchestrating or extracting model is a Covered Model the privacy notice must state 30-day provider retention. ZDR eligibility for the cheaper extraction model must be checked per model, and the Files API and Batch API are stateful regardless.
- Supabase Vault is often described as 'encryption for your data'; it is a project-level secrets store whose root key Supabase holds and whose decrypted view is readable by any role granted access. It is not per-tenant envelope encryption and a database dump plus the Management API key defeats it.
- Certificate pinning and jailbreak detection are commonly assumed to be 'must-haves' for a finance-adjacent app; UK CREST testers include pinning and root-detection bypass in every mobile engagement, they add pentest days and support tickets, and MASVS treats RESILIENCE as an additional profile for apps facing motivated binary attackers. For a consumer app whose real controls are server-side, they are optional at 10k households, not day one (inference).
- Intruder's headline prices vary by source ($119-$149 Essential, $239-$299 Cloud, $399-$499 Pro per month) because the page is a calculator with base fee plus per-target fees and VAT excluded; budget from the vendor quote, not blog figures.
- The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (in the Lords, Aug 2026) is sometimes cited as a coming duty on all UK apps; it applies to operators of essential services, relevant digital service providers, MSPs, data centres and load controllers. UK GDPR/DUAA and PECR are the laws that bind this product, and the DUAA's 19 June 2026 complaints-procedure duty is the new obligation most small apps have not noticed.
- ICO 'put beyond use' for backups dates from 2012 guidance under the 1998 Act and is still the working position for UK GDPR erasure; it only holds if you tell people about the backup window and actually age backups out on a fixed schedule.

## Can switching and affiliate commissions fund a free UK household-bills app? Unit economics, September 2026

**Recommendation.** Do not rely on commissions to fund the product. Launch with a free core and a low household premium tier from day one, and treat affiliate income as a bonus stream that you partly pass back to users (the Nous pattern), not as the P&L.

**Contested points.**
- '17m+ Open Banking users in January 2026' overstates: OBL reports 17.51m 'user connections' (18.81m by June 2026), and its methodology note says it cannot de-duplicate people who connect through more than one provider, so the unique-user figure is lower.
- 'About 28m UK households' is slightly low: ONS puts it at 29.0m for 2025 (published April 2026).
- The assumption that competitors like Nous live on commissions is wrong: Nous says it earns from Premium subscriptions (often employer-paid) and passes provider commissions back to members as cash rewards; Snoop and Emma are subscription-first too.
- Energy commissions are back, but at about GBP 20 per fuel - roughly half the 2016 level of about GBP 60 per dual-fuel switch - and the cheapest fixes are only ~7% under the cap, so the pre-2021 auto-switching economics have not returned.
- Insurance is not the lucrative affiliate vertical it looks like from the comparison sites' own margins: MoneySuperMarket pays affiliates GBP 3.50-4.20 with 50-60% declines and a 1-day window, and doing anything cleverer than a bare link puts an unregulated app inside the FCA perimeter.
- A GBP 1-3 per household per year cost model omits Open Banking data fees, which at aggregator list prices are GBP 3-19 per connected household per year; Little Birdie cited exactly this cost when it introduced a paid tier.
- Most of the engagement that renewal reminders produce is re-contracting with the existing provider (Ofcom: the 2019-20 rise in engagement was 'largely driven by people re-contracting'), which pays nothing to an affiliate.
- Look After My Bills and Switchd are not evidence that free-plus-commission works; LAMB died when commissions vanished in 2021-22 and Switchd charged a subscription.
- Alfred is often listed as a competitor but is a different animal: a GBP 195-a-year membership utility that supplies energy, broadband, mobile and insurance itself, with 50,000 members as its stated breakeven.

**Findings.**

## Reminder delivery infrastructure, cost and lawfulness (push, email, SMS, WhatsApp, calendar, scheduling, PECR, regulator timing conventions) plus accessibility obligations and definition-of-done for a UK household admin app built on Expo/React Native and Postgres

**Recommendation.** 1. Channel mix and fallback ladder (per household member, per message type, all state stored in Postgres):

**Contested points.**
- 'Expo handles web push from the same codebase' is wrong: Expo's own guide says 'Expo notifications do not support web notifications'; the web build needs a separate VAPID service worker and its own subscription table (and iOS Safari web push only works for Home Screen web apps - inferred, not verified this session).
- 'SES is $0.10 per 1,000' is only true a la carte; new SES accounts created from 21 July 2026 land on the Essentials plan at $0.16 per 1,000 unless you switch.
- 'A reminder that mentions comparing deals is still a service message' is not the ICO's position: any significant promotional material makes the whole message direct marketing (the Halfords fine turned on exactly this), and since 5 February 2026 the maximum PECR fine is £17.5m or 4% of turnover.

- 'The UK requires sender-ID registration' overstates it: Ofcom rejected a central registry in July 2026, but from 15 July 2027 aggregators must KYC senders and corroborate sender IDs, protected IDs need pre-registration today, and generic IDs such as 'Reminder', 'Bill', 'Energy' and 'Mortgage' are already blocked by UK operators - so the sender ID must be the brand name.

- 'The Equality Act only bites for public bodies' is wrong: s.29 covers any service to the public for payment or not, and the EHRC's 2026 statutory Code says the obligation applies to services on a website; the Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations are the only place WCAG is named in UK law, which is why WCAG 2.2 AA is the benchmark rather than a statutory rule.
- 'The European Accessibility Act applies the moment an EU user signs up' - Article 4(5) exempts microenterprises providing services (under 10 staff and under EUR 2m), so a one-to-three person company is exempt; the trap is outgrowing the exemption with an inaccessible product.
- 'Use react-native-accessibility-engine for tests' - it has not been released since November 2022; eslint-plugin-react-native-a11y, Testing Library role queries, Maestro and Xcode's performAccessibilityAudit are the maintained options.
- 'Inngest gives 50k free runs' - the free tier is 50k executions and every step counts, so a 3-step reminder workflow uses 4 executions per run.
- 'Schedule at 9am with cron' - pg_cron schedules are in GMT; the app must convert Europe/London (BST/GMT) itself or the reminder time shifts by an hour for half the year.
- WhatsApp UK utility pricing is not settled in the sources: third parties transcribe the Meta card as $0.0220 in USD but £0.0159 and £0.0174 in GBP; and from 1 October 2026 in-window replies stop being free.
- Sending every reminder by SMS looks affordable at 1k households (~£250/month) but scales to ~£25,000/month at 100k, which is why the brief's instinct that SMS is premium-only is right.

(for payment or not)' from discriminating, s.20 imposes the three-part reasonable-adjustments duty (provision/criterion/practice, physical features, auxiliary aids) which for services is anticipatory, and the EHRC's 2026 statutory Code of Practice states in terms that 'The obligation also applies to the provision of services on a website (section 29)' and recommends an access audit 'by a suitably qualified person'. [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/equality-act-2010-code-of-practice-for-services-public-functions-and-associations-2026/equality-act-2010-draft-code-of-practice-for-services-public-functions-and-associations-2026
- The EU European Accessibility Act (Directive 2019/882, enforceable from 28 June 2025) would apply to an app sold to EU consumers, with EN 301 549 referencing WCAG 2.1 AA, but Article 4(5) exempts 'microenterprises providing services' (fewer than 10 staff and no more than EUR 2m turnover or balance sheet) from the service requirements - so a one-to-three person company is out of scope until it grows, and building to WCAG 2.2 AA now removes the cliff edge. [high] - https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj
- Apple and Google set the concrete numbers to test against: Apple's Accessibility Nutrition Labels (App Store Connect, visible on iOS 26) are voluntary today but 'over time, you'll be required to share accessibility support details to submit new apps and app updates'; a label may be claimed only if every 'common task' (first launch, login, purchase, settings plus primary functions) works with that feature, Larger Text means text scaling to at least 200% without truncation, and misleading labels breach App Review Guideline 2.3; Android guidance requires 48x48dp touch targets, 4.5:1 contrast for text under 18sp (3:1 otherwise) and TalkBack/Accessibility Scanner testing; WCAG 2.2 AA adds 24x24 CSS px minimum targets (2.5.8) and 200% text resize (1.4.4). [high] - https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels/
- React Native accessibility tooling in 2026 is good enough to be a CI gate for AI-written code, but choose carefully: eslint-plugin-react-native-a11y is actively maintained (v3.5.1, Nov 2024, 'Active: Nearform is actively working on this project', ~165-230k weekly downloads); react-native-accessibility-engine has not been updated since November 2022 (v3.2.0) and should not be relied on; Maestro drives iOS and Android through the accessibility tree so a flow that cannot find an element by accessibilityLabel/contentDescription or testID is itself an accessibility failure; Deque's axe DevTools Mobile supports React Native testing and linting (pricing on request). [high] - https://www.npmjs.com/package/eslint-plugin-react-native-a11y
- UK audit prices: AbilityNet's Digital Accessibility Review is a fixed £4,950 + VAT covering up to 10 key pages, components or features with screen-reader testing, critical WCAG 2.2 failures, an insight report and help drafting an accessibility statement; the Digital Accessibility Centre sells WCAG 2.2 AA technical audits plus disabled-user testing on G-Cloud 14 at £340 to £1,350 per day (consultancy £680/day); Nexer Digital offers 'Small/Medium/Large' WCAG 2.2 packages and Hassell Inclusion two audit types (Live and Standard) without published prices; US per-screen benchmarks are $75-$250 per mobile screen. [high] - https://abilitynet.org.uk/accessibility-services/Digital-Accessibility-Review
- Competitors publish no lead times: Nous says only that it reminds you 'when a contract is coming to end' and reserves WhatsApp chat support and extra household logins for Premium; Snoop offers a 'weekly reminder of upcoming bills' plus 'contract renewal reminders'; the Confused.com app sends reminders for tax, MOT and insurance dates from the registration number and stored policy details. [medium] - https://www.nous.co/faqs

## Price-reference data for a UK household admin app: sources, cost, cadence, licence, and what may lawfully be shown outside a switching journey (energy, telecoms, insurance, mortgage, address and property, savings-claim compliance)

**Recommendation.** Build the comparison engine on the free OGL layer plus the user's own regulated notices, and treat paid tariff feeds and insurance/mortgage hand-offs as separately negotiated additions.

**Contested points.**
- The brief's 'SLC 22C, 42-49 days' is only partly right: Ofgem's 2019 customer communications reforms replaced the fixed 42-49 day end-of-contract notice with a principles-based 'appropriate time' rule in SLC 31I; the 42-49 day window survives in SLC 22C.5 only for suppliers proposing a further fixed term. The hard rule to mirror is the 49-day no-exit-fee switching window in SLC 24.
- No stable CSV/JSON download URL for the Ofgem regional tables was verified; the page's download menu is client-side and Ofgem does not publish historic regional caps on the site (they came out via FOI). Plan on parsing the per-period Annex 9 XLSX rather than assuming an API.
- Tariff Interoperability Phase 1a is not a general-purpose tariff API for household apps: it is electricity only, framed for optimisers and smart appliances, and the endpoint discovery mechanism for third parties is not documented. It is also February 2027, so it cannot be an MVP dependency.
- For home and motor insurance the 'renewal above new-customer price' hook is structurally weak because ICOBS 6B has forbidden it since January 2022; the real value is prompting a re-quote, which is regulated arranging when paid on conversion.
- OS Places API is often described as covered by the OS Data Hub's free £1,000 monthly premium allowance; it is explicitly excluded and costs £0.0282 per transaction with a Royal Mail component paid quarterly in advance, so Ideal Postcodes is cheaper at MVP volumes and includes UPRN.
- findAddress.io is not a PAF alternative: it states it does not use or supply PAF and relies on OGL data, so it avoids the Codeberry risk but is not equivalent in coverage.
- getAddress.io's status is settled: it ceased operations at 4pm on 4 February 2026 following the October 2025 judgment; a homepage that still renders is irrelevant.
- DVLA's VES API cannot be assumed for the car record: registration is closed for system upgrades as of 3 September 2026. DVSA MOT history is the live route; tax status will have to wait or be user-entered.
- Selectra's API is a per-market electricity price-planning service (time bands, feed-in, wholesale-indexed offers), not a gas plus electricity whole-market comparison feed; its headline 'from 1 EUR/month' tiers do not include the 2,500 EUR setup fee.
- The VAT removal on electricity from 1 October 2026 breaks naive period comparisons and also breaks naive tariff-versus-cap comparisons where a fixed tariff was quoted inclusive of VAT before October; Ofgem's own tables now show electricity excluding VAT and gas including it.
- Council tax bands: there is no lawful bulk or API source; Homedata's product is built by scraping the VOA. After Codeberry, treating a scraped Crown database as a supply chain input is a legal exposure, not a convenience.

**Findings.**
- Ofgem price cap data is quarterly, OGL-licensed, published as HTML tables with a per-table download menu plus per-period XLSX models; no stable CSV/JSON URL was verified and historic regional rates were only obtainable by FOI. [high] - https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/energy-regulation/domestic-and-non-domestic/energy-pricing-rules/energy-price-cap/energy-price-cap-default-tariff-levels
- From 1 October 2026 electricity has no VAT until 31 March 2027; Ofgem states costs cannot be compared directly with previous periods, so any period-over-period comparison must be VAT-normalised. [high] - https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/news/changes-energy-price-cap-between-1-october-and-31-december-2026
- Ofgem's typical domestic consumption values changed on 1 July 2026 to 2,500 kWh electricity and 9,500 kWh gas (from 2,700 and 11,500); savings estimates should state these or the household's own usage. [high] - https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/consultation/review-typical-domestic-consumption-values
- Energy rules to mirror: no exit fee for a switch in the 49-day window before a fixed term ends (SLC 24), and the renewal statement window of 42-49 days survives in SLC 22C.5 for renewals, while general notice timing became principles-based (SLC 31I) in 2019. [high] - https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2018/09/annex_1_statutory_notice_-_electricity_supply_licence.pdf
- Whole-market energy tariff feeds are commercial and account-managed; only Selectra publishes prices, and only Switchcraft explicitly welcomes use of its data outside a switching journey. [medium] - https://switchcraft.co.uk/energy-api
- Octopus Energy's products endpoints are public and unauthenticated, giving regional unit rates and standing charges with history; no published terms or rate limit beyond fair use. [high] - https://developer.octopus.energy/rest/guides/endpoints
- Tariff Interoperability Phase 1a goes live 18 February 2027: electricity suppliers must expose public tariff pricing data via standardised APIs with no consumer consent; the spec is baselined but the framework targets optimisers and smart appliances and electricity only. [high] - https://retailenergycode.co.uk/tariff-interoperability-arrangements-published/
- Ofcom requires end-of-contract notifications 10-40 days before the commitment period ends and annual best-tariff notifications thereafter, each listing the provider's best tariffs including new-customer prices; since 17 January 2025 new contracts must state future rises in pounds and pence with dates. [high] - https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-2-6-weeks/130197-helping-consumers-get-better-deals-on-their-broadband/ecn-consultation-and-statement/statement-helping-consumers-get-better-deals.pdf?v=323929
- There is no open, licensable live feed of broadband and SIM-only deals; Ofcom's pricing data is annual averages, deal feeds come only via partner APIs (stickee, Decision Tech, Uswitch) or affiliate/white label, and scraping PCWs is a database-right and terms risk. [medium] - https://www.stickeecomparison.co.uk/api-access/
- TV licence is £180 (colour) and £60.50 (black and white) from 1 April 2026, rising with CPI each April to the end of the Charter in 2027; MOT history is available free via the DVSA API, but DVLA's Vehicle Enquiry Service API is closed to new registrations as of 3 September 2026. [high] - https://developer-portal.driver-vehicle-licensing.api.gov.uk/apis/vehicle-enquiry-service/vehicle-enquiry-service-description.html
- For home and motor insurance the FCA's pricing rules mean a renewal price cannot lawfully exceed the equivalent new-business price, so 'you are paying more than a new customer' is a weak signal; market context comes from the ABI tracker (quarterly, free) and Consumer Intelligence (commercial). [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/icobs6b/icobs6bs2
- Any affiliate or white-label hand-off to insurance where commission depends on conversions is regulated 'arranging'; the app needs FCA authorisation or an (Introducer) Appointed Representative arrangement, as Nous and Seopa's partner model show. [high] - https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5
- Mortgage reference rates are free and OGL: Bank of England quoted household rates (e.g. IUMBV34, 2-year fixed 75% LTV) are monthly, published on the 5th working day of the following month; generic rate comparisons stay outside regulated advice under PERG 4.6, but 'best' or naming a product does not. [high] - https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/details/further-details-about-quoted-household-interest-rates-data
- Royal Mail's database right in PAF is enforced: Codeberry (getAddress.io) lost at trial on 10 October 2025 with additional damages for knowing or reckless infringement and ceased operations on 4 February 2026; the PAF End User Terms permit capturing and confirming addresses but not building a product that relies on PAF lookups, copying the data, or giving third parties access to extracted data. [high] - https://8newsquare.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/IDDQD-Judgment.pdf
- PAF-licensed lookup for a small app costs roughly 2.5p to 7p per lookup; Ideal Postcodes (UPRN and rooftop geocode included) is the cheapest fully licensed option at MVP volumes, OS Places API is 2.82p with no free allowance, and findAddress.io is not PAF at all. [high] - https://ideal-postcodes.co.uk/pricing
- Free property identifiers and open datasets: OS Open UPRN (OGL, six-weekly), HM Land Registry Price Paid Data (OGL, monthly, UPRN lookup table from 28 Aug 2026, but address fields restricted to personal/non-commercial use or residential property price services), Ofwat water company boundaries (OGL shapefiles), and the EPC register now behind GOV.UK One Login with a bearer-token API. [high] - https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/price-paid-data-downloads
- Council tax bands have no official API or bulk file: the VOA refused an FOI for the full valuation list, the GOV.UK 'Check and challenge your Council Tax band' service is manual, and Homedata's council_tax_band endpoint is built by scraping the VOA with a live fallback, which carries copyright, database-right and terms-of-service risk. [medium] - https://homedata.co.uk/pricing
- Savings copy is governed by DMCC s226 misleading actions (price, manner of calculation, 'specific price advantage'), CMA207/CMA209, ASA practice on 'up to' claims, the Ofgem Confidence Code and Ofcom accreditation; an 'up to' figure needs a significant proportion of users able to achieve it and the basis is material information. [high] - https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/british-gas-services-ltd-a25-1295064-british-gas-services-ltd.html
- Third-party price cap mirrors are OGL re-users, not Ofgem-licensed feeds: pricecaprates.co.uk is published by a comparison affiliate and energy-stats.uk is a hobby site built on the Octopus API. [medium] - https://pricecaprates.co.uk/
